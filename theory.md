Shear strength is the maximum resistance offered by soil against shearing stresses developed along a potential failure surface. It is one of the most important engineering properties of soil because it governs the stability and load-carrying capacity of geotechnical structures.

The shear strength of soil arises from two components:

1. Cohesion, which represents the bonding forces between soil particles.
2. Internal friction, which results from the friction and interlocking between particles.

The shear strength of soil is commonly represented by the Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion:

$$
\tau=c+\sigma\tan\phi
$$

where,

- $\tau$ = shear strength of soil,
- $c$ = cohesion,
- $\sigma$ = normal stress,
- $\phi$ = angle of internal friction.

The relationship between shear stress and normal stress is represented by a straight line known as the failure envelope.

### Importance of Shear Strength

The shear strength of soil is important for:

- Foundation design.
- Slope stability analysis.
- Retaining wall design.
- Earth dam construction.
- Embankment stability.
- Bearing capacity calculations.

Knowledge of shear strength helps engineers predict the behaviour of soil under loading conditions.

### Principle of the Direct Shear Test

The Direct Shear Test is used to determine the shear strength parameters of soil by applying a horizontal shear force to a soil specimen under a known normal load.

The soil sample is placed inside a split shear box. A vertical load is applied to produce the required normal stress, and the specimen is sheared along a predetermined horizontal plane until failure occurs.

The normal stress is calculated as:

$$
\sigma=\frac{P}{A}
$$

where,

- $P$ = applied normal load,
- $A$ = corrected area of the specimen.

The shear stress at failure is given by:

$$
\tau=\frac{F}{A}
$$

where,

- $F$ = shear force at failure,
- $A$ = corrected area of the specimen.

By conducting tests under different normal stresses, the cohesion and angle of internal friction can be determined.

### Factors Affecting Shear Strength

The shear strength of soil depends on:

- Soil type.
- Water content.
- Density.
- Drainage conditions.
- Magnitude of normal stress.

Different soils exhibit different shear strength characteristics under varying field conditions.

### Engineering Applications

The Direct Shear Test is widely used in:

- Foundation engineering.
- Stability analysis of slopes.
- Design of retaining structures.
- Earth dam construction.
- Pavement engineering.
- Estimation of bearing capacity.

Since shear failure is one of the most common modes of soil failure, the determination of shear strength parameters is an essential part of geotechnical investigations.
